# 1.6 AI overhaul: design proposal

Revision 1, approved by the owner and built in 1.6. Differences from the plan:

- Commands don't move their home forward (section 4): the rules let only a command that lost its home move, for
  players too, and the owner decided to keep that rule. Campaign fleets hold at the captured colony and
  troop ships come to them there instead.
- Debt (section 8): the "lost a quarter of its colonies in 20 turns" rule needs a history the AI doesn't keep; the
  shortfall rule covers collapsing empires. Cloaked packs (section 6) hunt mining outposts only, not transports or
  blockades (roadmap).
- Found along the way: the production planner counted only taxes as income, so empires stopped building while
  their treasury grew; it now counts mining, food sales and all upkeep, and at war banked credits pay for warships.
  Raider packs also take troop ships along and land on the weak colonies they hit.

## 1. Goal

1.5's command rules roughly halved conquest in all-AI games (invasions 5.4 → 2.5 per small game) and hit raiders and
warlords hardest (5% wins each in small games). The AI also ignores several tools it has had since 1.1 and 1.4. This
round teaches it:

1. Strikes launched from several commands at once.
2. Spare officers forming second fleets.
3. Campaigns that push on to the next target.
4. Minelayers and cloaked raiding.
5. Smarter use of the 1.4 special weapons.
6. Faster scrapping for collapsing empires deep in debt.
7. Outpost conversions (new in 1.6).

The other 1.6 additions need nothing new: personality bonuses apply on their own, the AI already sets food focus
only where it can farm, and it already scores food buildings, so it will build the Hydroponic Complex.

**Proposed target** (say if you want another): invasions back to about 3.5-4 per small game and 7-8 per medium
game, halfway back toward 1.4; raiders and warlords at 10% wins or more in small games; no rejected AI orders; no
race standing out. Measured with the usual sims on the same seeds (60 small games of 150 turns, 20 medium games
of 200 turns), plus a Total domination probe for the debt rule.

## 2. What the AI does today (the gaps)

- **One staging point.** `attack()` picks the ready command nearest the target and sends every warship there.
  Other commands' homes are only gathering points.
- **One fleet per strike.** The strike fleet takes the command's points (12 plus population and techs, so 14-20
  for most of a game). Groups that don't fit wait at staging as groups, and groups can't enter enemy space. Nothing
  forms them into a second fleet, even when the command has a spare officer (Officer Academy, Combined Fleet
  Doctrine, and now the raider bonus). This is the main reason strikes are small.
- **No follow-through.** After a capture the fleet sits at the new colony and is called back to staging next turn.
- **Minelayers** are never built. **Cloaking** is used only by commando infiltrators. **Hunter-killer satellites**
  are never built (the building scorer gives satellites no value).
- **Designs** pick the single best gun by damage × accuracy × shots and ignore specials: pierce, ion, pin, nanite,
  area and phase weapons are valued like plain guns or skipped.
- **Debt:** it disbands one weakest fleet a turn, and only once credits fall below -60 (or -20 with no threat).
  An empire losing colonies keeps most of its fleet for dozens of turns.

## 3. Strikes from several commands, and second fleets (items 1 and 2)

### Forming

At every ready command home (not only the one nearest the target), idle warships form fleets:

| Situation at a command home | What happens |
|---|---|
| A fleet is formed here with points to spare | Groups merge into it (as now), until it is strong enough (needed × 125%) |
| Groups left over, a free officer, points left | The strongest group forms a **second fleet**, trimmed to the points left |
| Groups left over, no officer or points | They stay as a group: home guard (`defend()` can use them inside our space) |

A second fleet goes along with the strike as an escort (each fleet keeps its own cohesion and flagship), or stays
home as a defense fleet when the empire is under threat (`posture: defend`).

### Converging

All drilled fleets (cohesion 80% or more) that can reach the target count toward the strike. Once their combined
strength beats what the target needs (known guards and colony defenses × nerve, as now), they launch **timed to
arrive together**: each fleet's travel time is worked out from its route and speed; the farthest launches first and
the others hold until their own travel time matches. The fleet carrying troops, and the bombers, go with the
group that arrives together.

| Knob | Proposed | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Launch cohesion | 80% (unchanged) | `AI_LAUNCH_COHESION` |
| Strength to launch | needed × 100% across all converging fleets | today one staging point must hold it all |
| Most commands joining one strike | all that can reach it within their supply | |
| Arrival window | same turn; at worst 1 turn apart | fleets commit to their course, so timing holds |

Raiders keep their packs (`raidPacks`), now from every command and with the extra officer each.

## 4. Campaigns (item 3)

After a capture (or when the target falls to someone else), a strike that is still strong does not go home:

| Condition | Proposed |
|---|---|
| Strength left | at least 75% of what the next target needs |
| Supply | the next target is within reach from the captured colony (it supplies once it is ours) |
| Cohesion | 60% or more (it doesn't need to re-drill to 80%) |
| Troops | carried troops cover the next target, or transports are sent to the captured colony instead of the old staging point |
| Next target | the war target's nearest known colony to the captured one |

If any condition fails, the fleet holds at the captured colony (now a command post, so it can take any order) and
reinforcements and transports gather there. When a captured colony is closer to the next target than the
command's home by two or more jumps, and no fleet of that command is in flight, the AI moves the command's home
to it (5 turns to get ready, as for a player).

## 5. Minelayers (item 4a)

| Who | Defense 5 or more (turtle, expansionist), and anyone under threat |
|---|---|
| How many | 1, or 2 for defense 7 or more; once Mine Warfare is known |
| Where | the colony most exposed to the war target (or to recent enemy sightings), then the combat outpost nearest the enemy |
| What they do | wait there laying mines up to the 60 cap (10 a turn each), then move to the next site; flee into the nearest colony if enemy warships appear |

Minefield buildings stay as they are (already valued). Hunter-killer satellites get a value in the building scorer
(4 shots × 6 damage ≈ a 25 hp gun platform), so exposed colonies build them.

## 6. Cloaked raiding (item 4b)

Raiders (and anyone with aggression 7 or more, once researched) build a **cloaked raider**: a destroyer or escort
hull (the cloak fits only hulls of up to 3 slots) with a Cloaking Device and the best guns. One warship in three is
a cloaked raider, up to 6.

Cloaked raiders form packs of 2-4 (they need a fleet to enter enemy systems) and go on **evade**, so they avoid
battle and slip past blockades. Targets, in order:

1. Rival **mining outposts** (any armed fleet in the system wrecks one unless it is guarded).
2. Rival **transports and supply ships** in flight or unescorted (they target transports already).
3. **Blockading** a lightly defended rival colony to cut its repairs and supply.

They avoid systems with known rival warships and return to resupply like any fleet.

**Decision needed:** today an empire remembers rival colonies it has seen, but rival outposts only while they are
in sensor range. To raid outposts sensibly the AI should remember them, which means a new list
`empire.outpostSightings` (like colony sightings; the player's map could show them too) and **state version 19**. I
propose adding it. Without it, raiders only hit outposts they happen to see.

## 7. Special weapons in designs (item 5)

Weapons are scored by what they really do against an average enemy at that stage of the game (one with shields of
about the empire's own best shield):

| Weapon | How it is valued | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Pierce (Gauss, Disruptor, Spinal Mass Driver) | full damage, ignoring the enemy's shields | main gun when it wins on that basis |
| Nanite Swarm | damage over 4 rounds, through shields | main gun candidate |
| Ion Cannon | no damage itself: worth the shield it strips for the other guns | 1 per 5 slots in line designs, if the enemy uses shields |
| Graviton Lance (pin) | 8 damage, and stops retreats | 1 in line designs for aggression 7 or more (warlords) |
| EMP Torpedo | knocks out parts | 1 in fortress designs (long range, opens the fight) |
| Singularity Charge (area) | damage × enemy fleet size (assume 4 ships) | 1 on battleships and dreadnoughts in line designs |
| Phase Torpedo | hunts support ships past screens | raider designs prefer it (they target transports) |
| Flak Battery | point defense | fortress designs, in place of Point Defense when better |
| Planet Cracker | destroys a colony | endgame drive only: one cracker dreadnought, used on a colony whose garrison is over 3× the troops the AI can bring and that has held out 20 turns |

Plus: hunter-killer satellites (section 5).

## 8. Debt (item 6)

| Rule | Today | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Starts | credits below -60 (or -20 with no threat) | credits below 0 **and** steady income minus upkeep below 0 |
| How much a turn | one fleet, the weakest | enough fleets (weakest strength per upkeep first) to cover the shortfall, at most a quarter of the fleet a turn |
| What goes first | weakest fleet | groups outside any command, then supply ships and spare transports, then fleets; never the last fleet guarding the capital |
| Collapsing empire | same | also when it has lost a quarter of its colonies in 20 turns: scrap down to what its income carries |

## 9. Outpost conversions (item 7)

| Conversion | When the AI does it |
|---|---|
| Mining → combat | a mining outpost within one jump of known rival colonies or recent rival warships, once a combat outpost is researched |
| Combat → mining (asteroids) | a combat outpost (not a depot, no marks) deep inside our space: 3+ jumps from any known rival colony, no enemy seen nearby for 30 turns, and mining researched |
| Combat outpost → habitat (gas giant) | gas giant habitats researched, outpost in our interior (as above), not a depot or marked: the habitat ship settles over it (counts as a colony target) |
| Habitat → outpost | never (the people are worth more) |

One conversion at a time, and only when the empire is not under threat; the outpost ship (or habitat ship) is built
for it like any other.
